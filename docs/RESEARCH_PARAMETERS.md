# ARC research parameter catalog

**63 intended criteria | Website-sales V1 | Public overview | October 9, 2026**

These criteria describe the planned research system. The initial release supplies instructions and specifications; live discovery, crawling, enrichment and audits are disabled. Provider-dependent checks are conditional on authorized access, budget and evidence availability. A missing value remains unknown.

## Market fit and eligibility

| Criterion | Evidence sought | Decision purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Industry fit | Law, accounting, construction, roofing, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, property management, surveying, engineering and related professional services. | Focus website-sales research on businesses the owner can serve. |
| Geographic fit | Country, city, metro and service territory; website projects can be delivered remotely worldwide. | Support configurable markets without assuming local presence. |
| Business identity | Company name, canonical domain and matching public address or entity identifiers. | Avoid combining evidence from different businesses with similar names. |
| Business size | Published staff information and approximate size bands; typically 1-100 employees, solo firms eligible. | Favor smaller businesses; flag uncertainty instead of inventing headcount. |
| Size boundaries | Configurable soft ceiling of 250 employees and absolute ceiling of 500. | Apply the agreed targeting limits using supported size evidence. |
| Operating status | Contemporary public signs of operation and confidently matched registry status where available. | Exclude confirmed closures; unavailable records remain unknown. |
| Excluded business types | Web/marketing agencies, franchises, major corporate chains, major hospital systems, major dealership groups and credit-card companies. | Avoid out-of-scope prospects before expensive research. |
| Duplicate and suppression checks | Canonical domain, business aliases, prior contacts, suppression and rejection records. | Prevent redundant research and unwanted outreach. |
| Source authorization | Provider permissions, public-access boundaries, attribution and retention requirements. | Use approved discovery paths and preserve source provenance. |

## Website design and usability

| Criterion | Evidence sought | Decision purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visual composition | Desktop/mobile screenshots showing alignment, hierarchy, balance, spacing and overall presentation. | Identify concrete design weaknesses with page-specific evidence. |
| Typography and readability | Text scale, line length, heading hierarchy and consistency. | Assess whether visitors can understand the offering quickly. |
| Brand consistency | Logo treatment, colors, image quality and consistency across sampled templates. | Distinguish fragmented presentation from coherent branding. |
| Visible layout defects | Overlapping elements, clipped headings, images covering text and cropped controls. | Prioritize directly observed usability failures. |
| Mobile responsiveness | Viewport configuration, horizontal overflow and layout behavior at representative narrow widths. | Find friction experienced by mobile visitors. |
| Mobile navigation | Menu behavior, visible service paths and access to contact actions. | Check whether key journeys remain usable on small screens. |
| Tap targets and reading size | Button/link spacing, control dimensions and legibility. | Identify barriers to completing a task on a phone. |
| Content freshness | Dated service information, expired banners, portfolio/blog dates and contradictory text. | Record relevant staleness without treating a quiet blog as a closed business. |
| Maintenance clues | Content dates, sitemap clues and other corroborating signals. | Estimate maintenance confidence; no precise redesign age from copyright alone. |
| Designer attribution | Agency footer or platform attribution considered alongside current quality. | A CMS brand or old designer credit alone is not a penalty. |
| Accessibility indicators | Contrast, headings, image alternatives, labels and keyboard barriers where tested. | Document observed issues and the limits of automated coverage. |

## Performance, SEO and conversion

| Criterion | Evidence sought | Decision purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Homepage reachability | HTTP responses, redirect behavior, TLS observations and timeouts. | Separate transient access failures from repeatable defects. |
| Representative page speed | Measured URL, device profile, timestamp and lab-tool output when enabled. | Avoid extrapolating one homepage test to an entire website. |
| Lab performance metrics | FCP, LCP, CLS and TBT when returned by the chosen audit tool. | Explain the scope of a controlled test. |
| Real-user performance | Field data, including INP, only when available from an authorized source. | Keep real-user observations separate from lab estimates. |
| Image efficiency | Resource dimensions, formats, oversized assets and loading behavior. | Locate likely performance improvements with specific resources. |
| Broken internal links | Bounded checks for 4xx/5xx responses, redirect loops and inaccessible destinations. | Expose broken visitor journeys without unbounded crawling. |
| Page metadata | Titles, descriptions, duplicate patterns and heading structure. | Assess basic search presentation; no promised ranking gains. |
| Indexing signals | Canonicals, robots directives, sitemaps and structured-data observations. | Record visible configuration issues with uncertainty. |
| Service clarity | Service pages, audience fit and explanation of what the business offers. | Evaluate whether the site communicates the actual offering. |
| Calls to action | Quote, call, consultation, booking and contact paths. | Find missing, ambiguous or inaccessible next steps. |
| Trust and credibility | Published testimonials, project examples, credentials and company information. | Assess credibility without inventing clients or endorsements. |
| Form usability | Visible fields, labels, layout and submission path; no automated prospect-form submissions. | Identify observable contact friction without sending messages. |
| Transport hygiene | HTTPS behavior, certificate/mixed-content observations and visible header signals. | Report bounded observations; no intrusive security testing. |

## Site structure and rebuild complexity

| Criterion | Evidence sought | Decision purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Canonical site origin | Normalized hostname, scheme and permitted subdomain scope. | Set clear research boundaries and prevent duplicate inventory. |
| Sitemap inventory | Discovered sitemap URLs and index entries. | Establish a bounded map of the public website. |
| Internal link graph | Observed links, parent/child relationships and path depth. | Show how visitors move between services, proof and contact. |
| Page classification | Home, services, locations, portfolio, projects, blog, team, contact, legal and other. | Organize an understandable sitemap and scope estimate. |
| Unique templates | Layout/structure similarity across pages, with confidence. | Distinguish 100 URLs from 100 unique designs. |
| Media inventory | Unique image references, repeated assets, galleries, video and document references. | Estimate content handling and migration work. |
| Business integrations | Booking, commerce, payments, chat, membership, embedded widgets and external scripts. | Identify features that require separate scoping. |
| Crawl coverage | Visited, discovered, blocked, failed and unexamined pages. | Show the limits of the audit rather than implying full coverage. |
| Offer suitability | Approximate page count, content readiness and integration complexity. | Qualify a simple five-page offer or route to a custom quote. |

## Business activity and spending signals

| Criterion | Evidence sought | Decision purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Published business scale | Staff bands, offices, service footprint and public project volume. | Estimate capacity signals with uncertainty; followers are not employees. |
| Advertising presence | Permitted evidence of current search/social advertising. | Treat existing acquisition investment as a spending signal. |
| Hiring activity | Dated public openings and company recruiting announcements. | Use current growth clues; discount stale reposts. |
| Expansion signals | New locations, services, offices, projects or credible announcements. | Support a growth hypothesis without claiming intent to buy. |
| Review volume | Permitted counts and review distribution. | Use as an imperfect scale/activity proxy, not verified revenue. |
| Review recency | Dates of recent feedback and freshness of activity. | Differentiate active demand from old aggregate totals. |
| Projects and commercial clients | Published portfolio work, client references, crews and fleet descriptions. | Look for observable operating scale and service demand. |
| Company news and public activity | Dated business updates, public posts and announcements where permitted. | Corroborate operation; missing social accounts are not a disqualifier. |
| Registry corroboration | Confidently matched legal entity, jurisdiction and current status. | Avoid applying a similarly named company's filing to the lead. |
| Industry context | Different spending proxies for professional services, trades and markets. | Retain a common score while interpreting signals in context. |
| Evidence confidence | Signal count, provenance, freshness, contradictions and unknown fields. | Separate estimated ability to spend from actual funds or purchase intent. |

## Contacts, evidence and action readiness

| Criterion | Evidence sought | Decision purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Published business email | Company-site contact, team and about pages; generic inboxes are acceptable. | Provide a legitimate business contact route without requiring a CEO email. |
| Decision-maker relevance | Owner, founder, partner, president, marketing or operations roles when supported. | Match outreach to a verified role when possible. |
| Name/title accuracy | Reliable source, correct business match and role date. | Prevent guessed names and false personal salutations. |
| Email provenance/status | Published, provider-sourced, pattern-derived, checked or unverified classifications. | Never present a guessed address as confirmed deliverable. |
| Alternative contact paths | Public business phone/address and other legitimate company routes. | Preserve useful options without making a phone mandatory. |
| Evidence record | Source URL, observed value, affected page, timestamp, device and confidence. | Make findings traceable and reusable across the pipeline. |
| Score reproducibility | Feature snapshot, weights, version, explanatory notes and unknown flags. | Explain why a lead ranked where it did. |
| Report readiness | Saved findings, screenshot references, scope and prioritized recommendations. | Generate a report from research already gathered. |
| Exact outreach approval | Owner-approved recipient and message; any change invalidates approval. | Keep the owner in control of every initial contact. |
| Stop conditions | Pause, suppression, reply, unsubscribe, bounce and other configured stop events. | Block unapproved actions and keep follow-ups disabled in the pilot. |

## Evidence rules

- Findings retain source, affected page, timestamp, test/device context, confidence and unknowns.
- Screenshots and bounded measurements support visual judgments.
- Lab metrics and real-user data remain distinct.
- A website platform, designer credit, absent social profile, generic email or old copyright alone does not disqualify a lead.
- Advertising, hiring and reviews do not establish private finances or willingness to buy.
- No intrusive security testing or automated contact-form submission is part of the review.
- Scores guide prioritization; recipient/content approval and sending permissions remain separate.
